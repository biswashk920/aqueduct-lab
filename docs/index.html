How to use this app

  1. Start with an example: press Aqueduct, Siphon aqueduct or Super-passage above the 3D view and look at the result badge on the right.
  2. Look around: drag the 3D view to rotate, scroll to zoom, or use Top / Side / Front. Use the exaggeration slider if the structure looks flat. Canal water is the strong blue, the drain (river) is the pale blue block, and white dots show flow.
  3. Change inputs on the left: try trough width 7 → 4 m and watch the drawdown, Froude number and cost plot react.
  4. Read the red boxes on the right: they flag choking, high Froude number, excess afflux and weak piers.
  5. Raise the high flood level (HFL): once it rises above the trough soffit minus 0.6 m the type changes to a siphon aqueduct. Lower the canal bed below the drain bed for a super-passage.
  6. Export: use the download buttons for CSVs, an inputs file you can reload in Python, and a PNG.

Glossary

Aqueduct
Canal in a trough above the drain's flood level; the drain flows freely beneath.
Siphon aqueduct
Drain flood level is above the canal bed, so the drain passes through closed barrels beneath the canal (barrels are drawn schematically here and not sized).
Super-passage
The reverse: the drain is carried over the canal in its own trough.
Afflux
Rise in upstream water level caused by the constriction.
Froude number
Velocity over wave speed; stay below 0.8 to avoid unstable near-critical flow.
Choking
The trough is too narrow to pass the discharge at the available energy, so water backs up.
Lacey regime
Empirical relations for stable alluvial channel width and scour depth.
Pier check
Screening only: axial stress = load per span / pier plan area, plus height-to-thickness ratio. Limits are editable inputs; it does not replace structural design.
